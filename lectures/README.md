## How a Lecture Is Laid Out

```text
lectures/LNN/
├── README.md              The lecture plan: agenda, what to do before, during and after.
├── appendix/
│   ├── a_….md             Theory. As many letters as the lecture needs.
│   ├── …_exercises.md     Always second to last.
│   ├── …_solutions.md     Always last.
│   └── images/            Figures, generated by `make diagrams`.
└── exercises/test/        The C++ suite that judges your toolkit.
```

**The appendix-letter rule is invariant across the course:** theory appendices come first, in
reading order, the exercises appendix is always second to last, and the solutions appendix is
always last. So a lecture with two theory appendices runs `a`, `b`, `c_exercises`, `d_solutions`,
and one with three runs `a`, `b`, `c`, `d_exercises`, `e_solutions`.

## The Exercises

Six to ten per lecture, each labelled with its kind:

* **Recall** checks that you can state a result without looking it up.
* **Hand calculation** is a number produced with a pencil, and it is where the approximations get
  made deliberately.
* **Design** starts from a requirement and ends with E12 component values.
* **Code** is a component of the toolkit, specified in prose, judged by the shipped suite.
* **Cross-check** closes every lecture, and there is exactly one.

**The Cross-check is the signature exercise.** You compute a number by hand, run your own
closed-form code on the same problem, and then solve the same circuit numerically with your own
nodal solver. Three legs, none of them authoritative on its own. Every Cross-check solution ends by
saying how large a discrepancy to expect and what each size of discrepancy would mean, because
"your two answers differ by 8 %" is the lesson rather than a failure.

**Solutions are published in full**, including the plausible wrong answer where there is one. This
is a self-study course and a reader with nobody to ask cannot be left with an unanswered exercise.

## The Test Suites

Every suite ships at least one test that needs no toolkit, so a suite you have not started reports
something rather than reporting red in silence. Every test for a class you write sits behind an
`#if __has_include`, so it switches itself on the moment the header appears, with no list to edit.

The suites are cumulative: every suite links your whole toolkit, so a change made in L10 that
breaks the solver you wrote in L01 fails L01's suite and names it.

```bash
export AEL_DIR=~/ael
make test                 # every suite
make test LECTURE=L07     # one of them
VERBOSE=1 make test       # show why a suite did not compile
```
