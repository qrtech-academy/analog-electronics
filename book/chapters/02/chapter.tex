\chapter{Reactance, phasors, and frequency response}
\label{c2:ch:reactance}

{\large\itshape The frequency domain, arrived at by making the solver complex rather than by
deriving a transform.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The capacitor and the inductor as devices that remember, and the time constant that says
    how long for.
  \item Sinusoidal steady state, and why a complex number can stand in for a sinusoid.
  \item Impedance: resistance that depends on frequency, and the two stamps that follow from it.
  \item Magnitude and phase, the decibel, and the Bode plot as two straight lines and a corner.
  \item The phase, which is the half people skip and the half that decides stability.
  \item The transformer, briefly, and where its model stops being true.
  \item The complex stamps and \code{ael::ac}.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

A resistor's current depends on the voltage across it now. That is the whole of
Chapter~\ref{c1:ch:circuits}. A capacitor's current depends on how the voltage has been
\emph{changing}, and that one difference is this chapter. Two devices with memory turn a circuit
from something with an answer into something with a trajectory, and the method of
Chapter~\ref{c1:ch:circuits} cannot solve it.

The way out is one observation rather than a transform. \textbf{In a linear circuit driven by a
sinusoid, every voltage and current in it is a sinusoid at the same frequency}, so only amplitude
and phase differ from node to node, and a single complex number carries exactly those two. Under
that substitution differentiation becomes multiplication by $j\omega$, a capacitor becomes a
conductance of $j\omega C$, and \textbf{nothing about the solver of Chapter~\ref{c1:ch:circuits}
changes except its scalar type}. The frequency domain costs about thirty lines, and only because
the first solver was written in a way that lets it.

What you get for them is the ability to read a response: the corner, the two asymptotes, and the
45 degrees of phase that are already there when the magnitude has barely moved. The magnitude says
whether a loop has gain left; the phase says whether that gain helps or hurts, and
Chapter~\ref{c4:ch:feedback} needs both.

Work \secref{c2:app:a} for the physics and the phasor argument, then \secref{c2:app:b} for the
stamps and the specification in \secref{c2:sec:b4}. The Cross-check that closes
\secref{c2:sec:exercises} is the first in this book where two legs are expected to disagree, and by
a factor of 6.6.
