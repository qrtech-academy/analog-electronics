\section{Review}
\label{c8:sec:review}

\needspace{6\baselineskip}
\subsection*{What you should be able to do now}
After this chapter, you should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Derive a follower's gain, input resistance and output resistance from the same model as Chapter~\ref{c7:ch:smallsignal}.
  \item Say what a follower costs and what it buys, in ohms rather than in adjectives.
  \item Compute what a given load does to a given stage, and choose between a follower, a Darlington and a redesign.
  \item State the three amplifier classes by what conducts when, and give the efficiency of each.
  \item Explain crossover distortion, and why it is worse than its size suggests.
  \item Size the emitter resistors of a class-AB stage from the idle current.
  \item Say why the bias generator must be thermally coupled to the output devices, and what happens if it is not.
  \item Carry every follower result to a MOSFET, and name the one thing that stops it being exact.
\end{itemize}

\needspace{6\baselineskip}
\subsection*{Questions to test yourself}
You should be able to answer:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A follower's gain is 0.974. Where did the other 2.6 per cent go, and what would you change to get it back?
  \item Looking into a follower's base with 8 ohms on its emitter, what do you see? Now say it again with the beta spread included.
  \item Why does a stage with 10 kilohms of output resistance lose 99.9 per cent of its gain into a loudspeaker, when the same load costs a follower only 3 per cent?
  \item Class B is 78 per cent efficient and class A is 25. Why is class A used at all?
  \item A class-AB stage idles at 120 mA. What emitter resistors does the 26 mV rule give, and what is the emitter factor it produces?
  \item The bias generator is two silicon diodes. Name the failure that follows from mounting them on the circuit board instead of the heatsink.
  \item A source follower's gain is lower than an emitter follower's at the same current. Give the number and the reason.
\end{enumerate}
