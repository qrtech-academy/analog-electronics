\chapter{Passive filters and the operational amplifier}
\label{c3:ch:filters}

{\large\itshape The first useful circuits, and the first active device.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item High-pass and low-pass RC, and the impedances they present on both sides.
  \item Cascading, and why two sections in a row do not give you the corner you designed.
  \item LC filters, resonance, and Q as two things at once: a sharpness and an overshoot.
  \item The band-pass, and the trade its two corners cannot escape.
  \item The operational amplifier as a black box, with two rules and nothing else.
  \item Inverting, non-inverting, buffer, summing and difference, all from those two rules.
  \item The comparator, hysteresis, and the Schmitt trigger, which are not amplifiers at all.
  \item Building \code{ael::filter}, the VCVS element, and \code{ael::opamp}.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

Swap the two components of an RC section and a low-pass becomes a high-pass; everything else about
them, including the corner, is the same. That is the first half of this chapter and it is
straightforward. The second half starts when you put two identical sections in a row and measure
the corner, and it is not the corner either section has: \textbf{the second section is a load on
the first}, and this is Chapter~\ref{c1:ch:circuits}'s arithmetic for the third time, in a circuit
where you cannot see it coming.

If loading is the problem, then something with a high input impedance and a low output impedance
between the sections is the answer, and that thing is an \textbf{operational amplifier}. It arrives
here as a black box with two rules --- no current into the inputs, no voltage between them --- from
which every standard configuration is one line of algebra. \textbf{You will use it on trust for
seven chapters and build it out of transistors in the last one}, which is the whole arc of this
book.

The rules are asserted here and derived in Chapter~\ref{c4:ch:feedback}, and
\secref{c3:sec:b4} is where they do not apply at all: take the feedback away and the circuit
that results behaves in a way the rules would call impossible.

\Secref{c3:app:a} is the passive filters, \secref{c3:app:b} is the amplifier and the
specification in \secref{c3:sec:b5}. The Cross-check in \secref{c3:sec:exercises} has four legs
rather than three, because the fourth one is the fix rather than another way of measuring the
fault.
